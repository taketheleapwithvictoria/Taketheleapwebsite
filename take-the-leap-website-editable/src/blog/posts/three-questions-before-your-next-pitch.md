Most pitch problems aren't slide problems. They start well before you open Canva, because the pitch was built without answering a few basic questions first.

Before I look at a single slide with a founder, I ask them these three.

## 1. Who is in the room?

Every pitch depends on its audience, so start by getting specific about who you're pitching to.

- **Customers** want to know what your product will do for them.
- **Investors** want to know whether your business can grow and give them a return.
- **Potential employees** want to know why your business is a great place to work.
- **Partners** want to know what's in it for them, and why working together makes sense.

These are four very different conversations. A pitch written for "everyone" usually lands with no one. Know exactly who's listening, and shape every part of your pitch for them.

## 2. What are you asking for?

Every pitch needs a clear ask. It's the reason you're pitching in the first place, and it should shape everything that comes before it.

If you're pitching for investment, work out exactly what that ask is: how much, and what it will help you achieve. If you're pitching to customers, be clear about what you're selling them and what you want them to do next. The same applies to employees and partners.

If you don't know what you're asking for, your audience won't know either, and the opportunity can pass without anyone saying yes.

## 3. What's the one thing you want them to remember?

Imagine I stopped someone on their way out of the room and asked them about your pitch. What would they tell me?

At pitch nights, judges and audiences often sit through several pitches in a row. By the end, most of the details have blurred together. If they remember only one thing about yours, what do you want it to be?

Once you've decided, make it impossible to miss. Build your pitch around it, give it pride of place on your slides, and pause after you say it so it has time to land. If you don't do this, your most important point can get lost among everything else.

You don't have to be the loudest or most polished pitch of the night. But you do need people to walk out knowing what your product is and why it matters.

## Get these right first

Slides are the easy part. When you're clear on who you're pitching to, what you're asking for and the one thing you want remembered, the rest of your pitch has something solid to be built on.
