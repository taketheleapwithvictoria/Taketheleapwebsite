Most pitch problems aren't slide problems. They show up long before Canva is even open. Here are the three questions I ask every founder before we touch a single slide.

## 1. Who, specifically, is in the room?

Not "investors" or "customers" in the abstract — the actual person or people you're pitching to. What do they already believe about your space? What have they said no to before? A pitch tuned to a generic audience is a pitch tuned to no one.

## 2. What's the one thing they need to remember?

If someone can only repeat one sentence about your pitch to a colleague afterwards, what should it be? If you can't answer that in one breath, the pitch doesn't have a clear spine yet, and no amount of slide design will fix that.

## 3. What's the objection you're avoiding?

Every founder has a question they're quietly hoping doesn't come up. That question is usually the most important thing to address head-on, early, in your own words, rather than waiting to be asked and looking caught out.

## Why this matters more than the deck

Slides are the easy part. Getting clear on these three things first means the deck you eventually build is in service of a clear argument, rather than a stand-in for one. It's the difference between a pitch that sounds polished and one that actually moves someone.
